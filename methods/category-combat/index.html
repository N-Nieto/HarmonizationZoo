Category-Combat: An Improved Multicenter Harmonization Algorithm Preserving Biological Variability between Nodule Types in Radiomics

Xiaolei Zhang, Xiaona Wang, Haihong Liu, M Iqbal Bin Saripan, Lijun Lu, Mohammad Hamiruce Marhaban, Xianling Dong

Research Square · 2026

↗ doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-7559470/v1↗ zhangxiaolei1993/Category_Combat

Abstract

Purpose: This study aims to develop an algorithm that mitigates radiomic feature inconsistencies introduced by multi-center imaging systems and heterogeneous acquisition settings, while retaining the biological variability between different nodule types. Materials and Methods: We developed the Category-Combat algorithm, which integrates a category-informed protection mechanism to enhance data harmonization. Unlike conventional methods that estimate the position centers of all radiomic features collectively, our approach groups feature by category and estimates them separately. Lung CT images from 1,789 patients were collected from three public datasets—LIDC-IDRI (1,012 cases), NSCLC-Radiomics (422 cases), and Lung-PET-CT-Dx (355 cases)—for external validation. A total of 93 radiomic features were extracted. The harmonization performance was assessed using Friedman's test. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and probability density distribution plots were used to evaluate the preservation of discrimination between feature categories, with statistical comparisons performed using the Wilcoxon test. Results: Friedman’s test showed that 87 of 93 radiomic features exhibited no significant differences after harmonization. PCA demonstrated effective removal of multi-center effects while maintaining distributional separation between feature categories. Density distribution plots and statistical results confirmed that the algorithm preserved discrimination between different nodule types. Conclusion: The Category-Combat algorithm offers a potential solution for building large-scale, multi-center radiomics datasets by harmonizing inter-center variations while preserving meaningful inter-category discriminations.
